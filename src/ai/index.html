My Stance

AI Generation is not used in the writing of my books. AI tools that attempt to usurp my creativity get thrown into the lake of fire and brimstone. AI tools that assist in research, brainstorming, or editing are acceptable.

My Thoughts

AI should never take the place of human creativity. It's a tool, and when used well, it can be very helpful. Here are some of my thoughts and experiences about when I did and didn't use AI.

To start, there are basic tools that writers already rely on, like spell check, grammar checks, and word counts. These tasks aren't very creative, but I've noticed that AI is now handling more of them. For example, Grammarly uses AI to suggest grammar changes, which I think is fine.

My problem starts when AI tools, or really the companies behind them, go beyond helpful suggestions and start generating new content. Suggesting better grammar is fine. Offering a different sentence structure is good too. But giving me text with ideas or events I didn't come up with myself is not okay. I'm emphatically against AI creating content.

I get that sometimes people want AI to generate content, especially for tasks that aren't creative. But when it comes to creative writing, that should stay with humans. I wish the companies making these AI tools understood the difference. I'd be much more comfortable using AI if I didn't have to worry about it adding things I didn't write.

As a software developer, I've tried out many AI tools. Here are some ways I've found them useful and appropriate.

When I started working on my book, I made an outline as a list. I wrote one main sentence for each chapter to create a rough timeline for the story. I'm sure many others do it this way too. I didn't use any AI for this part.

Next, I elaborated each sentence into a rambling summary of what happens for that chapter. Something like a brain dump of details and events I want to happen.

One of the areas that AI excels at is summarization. Since summarizing requires reducing information instead of adding to it, I felt this was a safe use. So, I asked AI to summarize each paragraph into story points. This worked great. The results were a set of story points for each chapter, based entirely on what I had provided. No AI generation.

After that, I expanded each story point and wrote several paragraphs myself. This felt like real writing to me. Having the next story point gave me a goal to work toward, though I sometimes changed things along the way, as most authors do. This process worked well for me.

I started with the free version of Grammarly and later upgraded to Grammarly Pro, which gave me access to their AI tools. Since my writing process was working, I didn't pay much attention to them until my first draft was completed. But one tool proved surprisingly helpful.

It was the humanizer tool. I knew humanizers are meant to hide when AI has been used to generate content, and I despise that idea. I still believe what I said before: AI should never replace human creativity.

I read an article about the ongoing back-and-forth between AI-generated content, detection tools, and humanizers. One interesting point was that humanizers have rules to stop them from adding new content, so they only work with what you give them. That made me wonder what would happen if I used a humanizer on something poorly written or hard to read.

I remembered that Grammarly had a humanizer tool, so I started playing with it. I tried it on all sorts of things, like passages from the Bible and The Silmarillion. Once I got past the fun of experimenting, I saw that the article was right; the rewording didn't add anything extra.

So I decided to try using it with my writing. It wasn't perfect by any means. It would sometimes suggest wording that technically said the same thing but wasn’t what I was aiming for. Wordsmithing was always needed. That being said, it would often spark my creativity and lead me to write something better than what I'd originally created. It's a bit ironic that a tool meant to hide AI use can instead improve authentic writing.

Here are a few other ways I found AI helpful:

  • I asked AI for Lexile or readability scores, which helped me spot parts that were too complex or too simple.
  • I asked AI to check for continuity and logic errors. It didn't find anything actionable, but it was reassuring since continuity was one of my biggest concerns.
  • I used AI image generation to prototype cover ideas. This helped me figure out what I wanted, and sharing these ideas with the artist I hired improved our communication.

In the end, AI is just a tool. It's great for analysis and feedback, but it should never take the place of human creativity.
